\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented an end-to-end pipeline for multi-view basketball player and ball
tracking and 3D reconstruction, evaluated on a three-camera recording from the
Sanbapolis facility.
Across the detection stage, domain fine-tuning of YOLOv26 with multi-pass inference
and class-agnostic NMS consistently outperforms both MOG2 and the COCO pre-trained
baseline, demonstrating that even a modest annotated domain-specific set is
sufficient to bridge the gap between general and sports-specific detection.
For tracking, the extended DeepSORT formulation---incorporating OSNet appearance
embeddings, ByteTrack low-confidence recovery, and track resurrection---improves
identity continuity over longer occlusions, though on this short clip the SORT
baseline remains competitive thanks to the identity-rich class labels produced by
the detector.
3D reconstruction via SVD-based triangulation followed by RTS-smoothed Kalman
filtering yields continuous per-player and ball trajectories; the dominant source
of residual error is manual extrinsic calibration bias rather than the
triangulation or smoothing procedure.

Future directions include replacing the manual calibration step with an automatic or
online method driven by tracked court line features, extending the evaluation to
full-game sequences that expose the tracker's long-term identity maintenance
behaviour, and investigating learned triangulation approaches that jointly optimise
detection confidence and geometric consistency across views.
