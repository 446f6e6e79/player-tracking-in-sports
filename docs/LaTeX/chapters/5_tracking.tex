\section{Tracking}\label{sec:tracking}

\subsection{SORT}
As a baseline tracker, we adopt SORT \cite{sort}.
Each active track maintains a Kalman filter whose state vector encodes bounding-box
position and velocity,
$\mathbf{x} = [u,\,v,\,s,\,r,\,\dot{u},\,\dot{v},\,\dot{s}]^\top$,
where $(u,v)$ is the box centre, $s$ its scale (area), and $r$ the fixed aspect
ratio.
At each frame, all tracks are propagated forward under a constant-velocity model,
and the resulting predicted boxes are matched to incoming detections by the
Hungarian algorithm on an IoU cost matrix.
Pairs with IoU below a gating threshold are forbidden from matching (max IoU
distance 0.8); tracks unmatched for more than 60 frames are deleted, and new
detections require two consecutive matches before being confirmed ($n_{\mathrm{init}}=2$).
The main limitation of SORT is that it relies exclusively on spatial proximity for
re-identification: when two players cross, their Kalman predictions may overlap, and
the tracker frequently swaps their identities.

\subsection{DeepSORT}
Our final tracker extends SORT with a deep appearance model \cite{deepsort}.
For each detected bounding box, OSNet \cite{reid}---a lightweight re-identification
network---extracts a compact embedding from the cropped image region.
Each track maintains a gallery of up to 100 recent embeddings; the cosine distance
between a new detection's embedding and a track's gallery mean serves as the primary
association cost.

Matching proceeds in a cascade ordered by track recency: the most recently observed
tracks are matched first using the appearance gate (maximum cosine distance 0.2),
and IoU serves as a fallback for tracks that are too new or for which the appearance
model is uncertain.
This ordering prevents a long-occluded track from claiming a detection through a
lucky spatial overlap.

We extend the baseline formulation with four robustness mechanisms:
(i) \textbf{NSA Kalman update}: the measurement noise covariance is scaled by each
detection's confidence score, so high-confidence boxes correct the state filter
more strongly than uncertain ones;
(ii) \textbf{ByteTrack second pass}: confirmed tracks left unmatched by the
appearance cascade are IoU-matched against the low-confidence detections retained
from the detection stage \cite{bytetrack}, recovering players during partial
occlusions without contaminating the appearance gallery;
(iii) \textbf{Track resurrection}: when a confirmed track is deleted, its appearance
gallery is retained for a short period; a new detection whose embedding is within
the cosine-distance threshold restores the original track identity;
(iv) \textbf{Coasting}: a briefly-unmatched track continues to emit its
Kalman-predicted box, provided it remains on screen and does not conflict with an
already-matched box, preserving trajectory continuity over short gaps.

After tracking, 2D trajectories are refined by linearly interpolating gaps of up to
five consecutive undetected frames, followed by an exponential moving average
($\alpha = 0.5$) to damp frame-to-frame jitter.

\subsection{Label Resolution}
Because the fine-tuned detector encodes player identity in the class label, a single
track may accumulate detections with slightly different class predictions across
frames.
For each track, a stable label is assigned by majority vote over all detections,
weighted by confidence score.
When two tracks claim the same identity, the track with the higher cumulative
confidence retains its label, and the other falls back to its next-highest-ranked
class.
The ball is exempt from this procedure, as it is a category rather than a unique
identity.
