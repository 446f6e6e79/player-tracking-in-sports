\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Background Subtraction}
Background subtraction is one of the earliest and most widely-used approaches for
moving-player detection in sports footage.
Manafifard et al.\ \cite{survey} survey the landscape of soccer player tracking,
noting that Mixture-of-Gaussians (MOG) models provide an effective and
computationally cheap baseline: each pixel's intensity distribution is modelled as a
weighted mixture of Gaussians that adapt over time, and pixels deviating
significantly from their background model are labelled foreground.
Subsequent variants improve robustness through shadow detection and adaptive
learning rates, but the fundamental assumption of a mostly static background
remains a limiting factor for indoor sports arenas with dynamic spectator regions.

\subsection{Object Detection with Deep Learning}
Redmon et al.\ \cite{yolo_paper} introduced YOLO, a single-stage detector that
reformulates object detection as a direct regression from image pixels to bounding
boxes and class probabilities via a convolutional backbone and a detection head.
The YOLO family \cite{yolo_github} has since been refined through numerous
iterations, improving detection speed and accuracy while maintaining real-time
performance.
Pre-training on large-scale datasets such as COCO endows these models with general
semantic representations that transfer well across domains, making fine-tuning on
small application-specific datasets a practical strategy.

\subsection{Multi-Object Tracking}
Bewley et al.\ \cite{sort} proposed SORT, combining a Kalman filter for motion
prediction with the Hungarian algorithm for detection-to-track assignment.
Despite its simplicity, SORT achieves competitive performance but suffers from
frequent identity switches when objects cross or become occluded.
Wojke et al.\ \cite{deepsort} extended SORT with a deep appearance metric based on
a lightweight re-identification network (OSNet \cite{reid}) that extracts compact
appearance embeddings; a matching cascade prioritising tracks by recency and
appearance similarity substantially reduces identity switches.
Zhang et al.\ \cite{bytetrack} introduced ByteTrack, which performs a second
association pass on low-confidence detections after the primary high-confidence
pass, further improving track continuity under partial occlusion.

\subsection{Multi-View 3D Reconstruction in Sports}
Multi-camera setups are standard in sports analytics to resolve occlusions and
recover three-dimensional player positions \cite{survey}.
Classical approaches pair known camera calibration parameters with epipolar
geometry or direct linear triangulation (DLT) to lift matched 2D detections into
3D, often followed by temporal smoothing to suppress noise introduced by detection
errors and imperfect calibration.
