MOG2 achieves the lowest $F_1$ on all cameras, confirming that the assumption of a
static background is violated by moving spectators and specular reflections.
The pre-trained YOLOv11 baseline generalises far better, but recall remains
limited because the ball is absent from its label set and some player detections are
missed in crowded regions.
The fine-tuned model with class-agnostic NMS achieves the best $F_1$ on cam\_4
(0.870) and competitive scores on the other views, with a notably higher precision
than the pre-trained model on cam\_4 (0.923 vs.\ 0.927) and substantially better
recall on cam\_13 (0.732 vs.\ 0.602), demonstrating the value of domain adaptation.

On IDF1, SORT outperforms DeepSORT on this short clip, while HOTA scores are close
between the two across all cameras.
The identity-in-class labels from the fine-tuned detector already encode player
identity at detection time, which benefits SORT's simple IoU matching.
DeepSORT's advantage lies in its greater resilience to prolonged occlusions through
appearance-based re-identification; however, over only 525 frames, such events are
relatively infrequent, and the increased track count from coasting and resurrection
slightly raises IDFP.
The association component (AssA) is broadly similar for both trackers, confirming
that the main tracking challenge on this clip is detection recall rather than
association quality.

Reprojection error is substantially lower for cam\_13 (median 28.9\,px) than for
the other two views (median 94.5 and 50.0\,px), reflecting the relative quality of
the manually selected calibration correspondences.
The large RMSE values for cam\_2 and cam\_4 indicate the presence of outlier frames
where the triangulated point reprojects far from the observed detection, likely due
to a poor extrinsic estimate or a detection on a mismatched identity.

The trajectory errors (ADE/FDE/MTE in the hundreds of pixels) are large across all
views, revealing that the primary bottleneck is not the tracking or triangulation
algorithms but calibration accuracy: a systematic offset in the estimated camera
pose propagates directly into a constant positional bias in the reconstructed
trajectories.
The fragment count is low (2--5 per camera), indicating that the reconstructed
tracks cover the annotated span with few coverage gaps.

\subsection{Limitations and Failure Cases}

Several systematic failure modes remain.
Prolonged occlusions (more than $\sim$2.4\,s at 25\,fps) exhaust the tracker's
maximum age, causing track deletions and subsequent identity re-assignment even with
appearance-based resurrection.
Ball detection is unreliable under severe motion blur or when the ball passes
directly under a player, reducing its visible area below the confidence threshold.
Partial court coverage by cam\_2 and cam\_4 means that a player near the court
boundary may be visible in only one or two views, reducing the robustness of
triangulation.
Finally, the manually determined extrinsic calibration introduces biases that
directly inflate the reprojection and trajectory errors reported above; an automatic
or online calibration procedure would be necessary for deployment.
