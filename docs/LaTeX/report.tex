\title{Player Tracking in Sports --- Multi-View Tracking and 3D Reconstruction}
\author{Andrea Blushi\inst{1} (ID: 264468) \and Davide Don\`{a}\inst{1} (ID: 264467)}
\institute{University of Trento, Master's Degree in Computer Science, Italy\\}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Reliable player and ball tracking in multi-camera sports footage is a prerequisite
for automated game analysis, yet it remains challenging due to rapid motion,
frequent mutual occlusions, and domain-specific visual characteristics such as
specular floor reflections.
We present an end-to-end pipeline applied to synchronized three-camera basketball
recordings from the Sanbapolis facility in Trento, Italy, covering detection,
multi-object tracking, and 3D reconstruction.
For detection, we systematically progress from classical background subtraction
(MOG2) through a pre-trained YOLOv11 baseline to a domain-fine-tuned YOLOv26 with
multi-pass inference and class-agnostic non-maximum suppression, achieving the best
detection F1 across all views.
For tracking, we compare SORT against an extended DeepSORT with OSNet re-identification,
ByteTrack low-confidence recovery, and trajectory smoothing, demonstrating improved
identity maintenance through player crossings.
3D player and ball positions are reconstructed per frame via SVD-based triangulation
across the three calibrated views and refined with an RTS-smoothed Kalman filter;
residual error is dominated by calibration noise and occlusion-driven gaps rather
than the triangulation method itself.
\end{abstract}
